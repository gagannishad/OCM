Explain the Following Terms

Complete Textbook Exercises & 160+ Extensive Custom Terms from Chapters 2, 4, and 8

PART A: Textbook Exercises

Chapter 2: Functions of Management

  • 1. Management: A set of principles which relate to the various functions such as planning, organizing, staffing, directing, coordinating, and controlling which are helpful in achieving organizational goals.
  • 2. Planning: The basic, fundamental function of management that involves deciding in advance what to do, how to do it, when to do it, and who is to do it. It is an intellectual process of logical thinking.
  • 3. Organizing: The process of defining and grouping the activities of the enterprise, bringing required resources together (men, money, material, machine), and establishing authority relationships among them.
  • 4. Staffing: The function of execution according to the organizational structure. It pertains to the recruitment, selection, development, training, and compensation of the workforce.
  • 5. Directing: The process of instructing, guiding, communicating, inspiring, motivating, and supervising the employees to achieve predetermined goals. It is the life spark of an enterprise.

Chapter 4: Business Services

  • 6. Transport: The movement of people, animals, and goods from one location to another, functioning as a means of carrying items and individuals across distances.
  • 7. Communication: An art of exchanging ideas, facts, and information from one person or entity to another using various mediums.
  • 8. Banking: A financial service involving an institution that deals with accepting deposits for the purpose of lending or investment, repayable on demand or otherwise.
  • 9. Insurance: A contract and means of protection from financial loss where the insurer agrees to compensate the insured against contingent or uncertain losses for a premium.
  • 10. Warehousing: An establishment and group of activities connected with the safe storing, accumulating, and preserving of goods from the time of production till consumption.

Chapter 8: Marketing

  • 11. Market: Derived from the Latin word 'mercatus', it means to trade or a place where business is transacted involving buyers, sellers, and the exchange of goods for money.
  • 12. Place Concept of Market: The common understanding of a market as a specific physical geographic location where buyers and sellers meet to exchange goods.
  • 13. Commodity Concept of Market: A concept that places emphasis strictly on the buying and selling of specific goods or services, regardless of the physical place of exchange.
  • 14. Digital Concept of Market: Also known as Space Market, it uses Information Technology (E-Commerce portals, mobile apps) for buying, selling, and communicating product features.
  • 15. Product: Goods or services that are offered to customers for sale, capable of satisfying a customer's needs. It can be tangible or intangible.
  • 16. Price: The specific monetary amount that a customer pays for a product, acting as a crucial factor in creating demand and determining profitability.
  • 17. Promotion: An element of the marketing mix involving tools like advertising and personal selling to communicate product features and encourage customers to buy.
  • 18. Marketing Mix: The combination of different marketing variables (Product, Price, Place, Promotion) that a firm blends and controls to achieve desired results from a target market.
  • 19. Packaging: The process of designing and creating wrappers, containers, or boxes to protect the product from damage and act as an attractive promotional tool.
  • 20. Labeling: The process of providing a slip on the product that contains all critical, necessary information regarding the product and its producer.

PART B: 140+ Extensive Exam Concepts

Chapter 2: Functions of Management

  • 21. Controlling: The process of comparing the actual performance with the predetermined standard performance, measuring deviations, and taking corrective steps.
  • 22. POSDCORB: A keyword coined by Luther Gullick for management functions: Planning, Organizing, Staffing, Directing, Co-ordination, Reporting, and Budgeting.
  • 23. Co-ordinating: The integration and synchronization of the efforts of a group to provide unity of action, acting as a hidden force binding all functions.
  • 24. Forecasting: A vital technique used during planning that looks into the future to anticipate changes and uncertainties to prepare preventive measures.
  • 25. Delegation: The assignment of specific activities and the provision of adequate authority to appropriate departments or managers.
  • 26. Specialization: The outcome of dividing work into distinct units in the organizing function, which increases the overall efficiency and expertise of an organization.
  • 27. Human Resource Development: Achieved heavily through the staffing function, it involves training and developing existing employees to treat them as an asset.
  • 28. Team Spirit: A sense of mutual co-operation and group effort fostered largely by directing and coordinating, vital for organizational success over individual efforts.
  • 29. Deviation: The difference or gap found during the controlling function when the actual performance does not align with the standard target.
  • 30. Standard Performance: Predetermined benchmarks set during the planning phase against which the actual performance is measured during controlling.
  • 31. Preventive Measures: Actions decided in advance during planning to tackle anticipated future risks and uncertainties.
  • 32. Optimal Utilization of Resources: Using all physical, financial, and human resources (men, money, machine) at their best capacity to avoid wastage.
  • 33. Appraising: A step in the staffing process involving the regular evaluation of employee performance to grant promotions and merits.
  • 34. Instructing: A core element of directing where managers issue clear orders and procedures to subordinates.
  • 35. Corrective Action: The final step in the controlling process implemented to fix deviations and ensure future alignment with goals.
  • 36. Unity of Action: Brought about by co-ordination, ensuring all departments harmonize their varied activities toward one common organizational goal.
  • 37. Top Level Management: The level of management primarily responsible for setting core organizational goals and drafting ultimate plans.
  • 38. Psychological Pressure: The awareness among employees that their performance will be evaluated in the controlling function, acting as a motivation to perform better.

Chapter 4: Business Services

  • 39. Services: Intangible, instantly perishable acts or performances offered for sale that do not result in the ownership of anything.
  • 40. Intangibility: The characteristic of services indicating they lack material physical form and cannot be touched or seen.
  • 41. Inseparability: The characteristic where the production and consumption of a service take place at the exact same time and cannot be separated from the provider.
  • 42. Inconsistency: The heterogeneous nature of services meaning quality may differ from time to time and perfect standardization is impossible.
  • 43. Perishability: The inability to store services as an inventory for future sale or use.
  • 44. Non-transferability: The characteristic indicating that the ownership of a service cannot be transferred to the customer.
  • 45. Central Bank: The apex financial institution of a country (like RBI) that issues currency notes and acts as a banker to the government.
  • 46. Commercial Bank: A bank that operates for profit by performing primary functions of accepting deposits and granting loans to individuals and industries.
  • 47. Co-operative Bank: Banks working at village, district, and state levels aimed at providing credit to farmers and economically backward people.
  • 48. Industrial Development Bank: Institutions providing medium and long-term funds to business firms for expansion and modernization (e.g., IFCI, SFC).
  • 49. Exchange Bank: Banks that facilitate foreign exchange transactions and finance foreign trade by issuing letters of credit.
  • 50. Regional Rural Bank (RRB): Banks sponsored by large public sector banks to mobilize rural deposits and provide loans to marginal farmers.
  • 51. Investment Bank: Banks providing financial and advisory assistance (like mergers and acquisitions) to corporate clients, without directly dealing with the public.
  • 52. EXIM Bank: Export and Import Bank, providing principal financial assistance for coordinating and promoting the country's international trade.
  • 53. SIDBI: Small Industries Development Bank of India, the principal institution for the promotion and financing of the MSME sector.
  • 54. NABARD: An apex institution concerned with policy planning and financing agricultural operations and rural development through other banks.
  • 55. Payment Bank: A newer model of banking offering remittance services and limited deposits (up to Rs. 1 lakh) but legally prohibited from issuing loans or credit cards.
  • 56. Fixed Deposit: A time deposit account where a fixed sum is kept for a predetermined period bearing a high, fixed interest rate.
  • 57. Recurring Deposit: An account for individuals with regular income where a fixed sum is deposited monthly, earning a high interest rate until maturity.
  • 58. Saving Account: A demand deposit account meant to encourage the habit of maximum savings among people earning regular salaries.
  • 59. Current Account: A demand deposit account operated by business firms with no restrictions on transactions and no interest paid, but offering an overdraft facility.
  • 60. Cash Credit: A short-term advance provided to current and savings account holders to meet longer working capital requirements.
  • 61. Overdraft: A temporary facility offered strictly to current account holders allowing them to withdraw more than their actual balance.
  • 62. Discounting of Bills: An advance where the drawer of a bill of exchange can obtain funds from a bank at a discount before the due date.
  • 63. Portfolio Management: An agency function where large commercial banks purchase and sell securities on behalf of their clients.
  • 64. Dematerialisation: A facility provided by banks to hold and electronically transfer a client's shares and securities in an electronic format.
  • 65. E-banking: Virtual banking where banking operations are completely computerized.
  • 66. ATM: Automated Teller Machine, operated by customers to electronically withdraw or deposit money at any time.
  • 67. Credit Card: A payment card where the issuing bank creates a revolving account and grants a line of credit to the user.
  • 68. Debit Card: A card where payments made by the user are immediately deducted directly from the account holder's available bank balance.
  • 69. RTGS: Real Time Gross Settlement, the fastest money transfer system where funds settle individually on a real-time basis.
  • 70. NEFT: National Electronic Fund Transfer, a system where transactions are bundled and settled in half-hourly batches on a deferred net basis.
  • 71. Insured: The person or entity who is financially protected against contingent losses (known as 'assured' in life insurance).
  • 72. Insurer: The firm or company that agrees to compensate the insured against losses for a specific consideration.
  • 73. Premium: The specific monetary consideration paid by the insured to the insurer on a timely basis for insurance coverage.
  • 74. Policy: The official statement of contract between the insured and insurer containing all terms and conditions of coverage.
  • 75. Principle of Utmost Good Faith: The rule requiring both the insurer and insured to completely and correctly disclose all material facts before signing the contract.
  • 76. Principle of Insurable Interest: The requirement that the insured must suffer a direct financial loss from the destruction of the subject matter.
  • 77. Principle of Indemnity: The guarantee to restore the insured strictly back to the exact same financial position they were in prior to the loss.
  • 78. Principle of Subrogation: The rule transferring ownership of scrapped or recovered property to the insurer once full compensation has been paid.
  • 79. Principle of Contribution: If multiple policies are taken, all insurers proportionately contribute to compensate only the actual loss amount.
  • 80. Principle of Mitigation of Loss: The strict obligation of the insured to take all possible active measures to save their property during an incident.
  • 81. Principle of Causa-Proxima: The rule stating that the nearest, most proximate direct cause of loss is considered to determine the insurer's liability.
  • 82. Whole Life Policy: A life insurance policy covering the entire life of a person, paying out only upon the death of the insured to the nominee.
  • 83. Endowment Policy: A life policy taken for a specific period, paying out upon maturity to the insured or upon death to the dependents.
  • 84. Term Insurance Policy: A policy covering a specific period with the lowest premium, paying out only if the insured dies during the term.
  • 85. Annuity Policy: A policy where the premium is paid as a lump sum, and the insured receives a specific periodic sum back, like a pension.
  • 86. Money-back Policy: A policy providing regular percentage payouts of the sum assured during the lifetime, plus the full sum upon death.
  • 87. Voyage Policy: A marine policy covering a vessel or cargo strictly for one specific voyage, irrespective of the time taken.
  • 88. Time Policy: A marine policy insuring subject matter for a definite period, not exceeding one year.
  • 89. Valued Policy (Marine): A policy where the exact value of goods is agreed upon beforehand to facilitate easy claims for items difficult to assess.
  • 90. Blanket Policy: A marine policy taken for a maximum limit of protection covering multiple risks or properties under one initial premium.
  • 91. Port Risk Policy: A marine policy covering all types of risks for a vessel while it is anchored safely at a specific port.
  • 92. Fleet Policy: A marine policy where several ships belonging to one single owner are all insured together under the same policy.
  • 93. Average Policy: A fire policy clause determining that if property is undervalued, the insurer only pays a proportionate percentage of the loss.
  • 94. Floating Policy: A fire policy taken for one sum covering fluctuating quantities of goods lying across several different godowns or locations.
  • 95. Reinstatement Policy: A fire policy where the insurer physically replaces the destroyed property instead of paying monetary compensation.
  • 96. Comprehensive Policy: A policy that covers a multitude of major risks under a single umbrella, such as fire, floods, and explosions.
  • 97. Consequential Loss Policy: A fire policy that provides indemnity for intangible financial losses, such as loss of net profits, following a disaster.
  • 98. Road Transport: A flexible mode of transport connecting people on land surfaces, offering cheap and door-to-door service over short distances.
  • 99. Rail Transport: A dependable mode of transport occupying an important place in carrying heavy, bulky goods and passengers over long land distances.
  • 100. Air Transport: The fastest and relatively most expensive mode of transport utilizing aircraft, crucial during emergencies and cross-border travel.
  • 101. Water Transport: The most economical mode of transport for bulky and heavy goods across oceans or inland waterways, utilizing natural routes.
  • 102. Monorail: A rapid transit system in urban areas where the track consists of a single rail or beam, heavily avoiding street traffic jams.
  • 103. Pipeline Transport: A transport network utilizing pipes to send liquids and gases (like water, sewage, or petroleum) over both short and long distances.
  • 104. Private Warehouses: Storage facilities owned and operated exclusively by big manufacturers or merchants to fulfill their own heavy storage needs.
  • 105. Public Warehouses: Specialized establishments working under government licenses providing storage facilities to the general public and small traders for a fee.
  • 106. Bonded Warehouses: Warehouses licensed by the government specifically to securely store imported goods until the importer clears the custom duties.
  • 107. Duty Paid Warehouses: Public warehouses usually located near docks used to store goods after customs duties are paid, often useful for re-exporting.
  • 108. Cold Storage Warehouses: Warehouses refrigerated at very low temperatures designed to safely preserve highly perishable commodities like dairy and fruits.
  • 109. Inland Letter: A specific postal communication on a folded sheet of paper ensuring message confidentiality for transmission strictly within India.
  • 110. Parcel Post: A postal service allowing items of specified sizes to be sent across the country with optional insurance for safety.
  • 111. Business Post: A service offering comprehensive mailing solutions including printing, collating, and inserting specifically for corporate companies.
  • 112. e-Post: A postal service where a message is electronically transmitted via the internet and printed out as a hard copy for final physical delivery.
  • 113. Courier Service: A premium delivery service provided by individuals or companies offering speed, tracking, and secure exchange of items.
  • 114. Internet: A global interconnected system of computer networks using the TCP/IP protocol suite to link devices and facilitate digital communication.

Chapter 8: Marketing

  • 115. Mercatus: The Latin word origin of 'market', literally translating to 'trade' or a place where business is transacted.
  • 116. Exchange Concept of Market: A concept viewing the market as an exchange of goods free from fraud, built upon mutual trust and free consent.
  • 117. Demand/Customer Concept: The perspective that defines the market as the total aggregate demand generated by potential buyers for a specific product.
  • 118. Local Market: A market where commodities are bought and sold strictly within the localized geographical limits of a region.
  • 119. National Market: A market covering commodities that are produced and sold extensively within the entire boundaries of a country.
  • 120. Very Short Period Market: A market existing only for a few hours or a day specifically for selling perishable goods like vegetables.
  • 121. Wholesale Market: A market focused on the bulk purchase and sale of goods in large quantities at cheaper prices to retailers.
  • 122. Retail Market: The market stage where retailers sell goods directly to the ultimate end-consumer in small, convenient quantities.
  • 123. Primary Market: A market heavily dealing with the exchange of raw primary products such as agricultural produce and forest goods.
  • 124. Terminal Market: The final market layer where finished goods are sold directly to the ultimate users or end consumers.
  • 125. Capital Market: A financial market designed specifically for the borrowing and lending of long-term capital with extended maturity periods.
  • 126. Regulated Market: Markets governed closely by statutory government provisions, such as Stock Exchanges or Commodity Exchanges.
  • 127. Perfect Market: A theoretical market structure with a large number of buyers and sellers trading identical products with perfect market knowledge.
  • 128. Imperfect Market: A market characterized by imperfections such as a single seller or a maladjustment in demand and supply.
  • 129. Monopoly: An imperfect market scenario featuring only one single producer or seller who dictates the entire supply and price.
  • 130. Duopoly: An imperfect market featuring exactly two sellers who share a monopoly over the sale of a homogeneous or differentiated product.
  • 131. Oligopoly: A market featuring only a few dominant sellers producing goods for a large number of buyers.
  • 132. Monopsony: A unique imperfect market situation where there is a single buyer of a commodity, dictating terms to multiple sellers.
  • 133. Consumer Awareness: A state achieved by marketing where potential consumers are educated and informed about new product utilities and features.
  • 134. Marketing Research: The function of meticulously collecting and analyzing information to assess what consumers want, in what quantity, and at what price.
  • 135. Buying and Assembling: The marketing function involving collecting raw materials from varying sources to a centralized place for production.
  • 136. Product Development: The continuous process of designing and upgrading product features, shapes, and quality to suit the changing needs of consumers.
  • 137. Standardisation: The process of predetermining standards regarding size, weight, and quality to ensure strict uniformity in a product.
  • 138. Grading: The physical classification of primary or agricultural products into groups according to similar quality, size, and shape characteristics.
  • 139. Trademark: A brand name, logo, or special identity that has been legally registered, ensuring no competitor can copy it.
  • 140. Branding: The process of assigning a unique identity, name, or symbol to a product to clearly differentiate it from competitive offerings.
  • 141. Customer Support Service: Post-sale services such as technical assistance and helplines designed to ensure total customer satisfaction and build loyalty.
  • 142. Pricing: The critical function of assessing costs, competition, and demand to determine the exact monetary value a customer pays for a product.
  • 143. Distribution: The comprehensive set of activities ensuring the efficient physical movement of finished goods from the factory to the consumer.
  • 144. Place (Marketing Mix): The strategic variable involving distribution channels and locations ensuring a product is easily accessible to buyers.
  • 145. People (7Ps): The human element comprising employees and staff who interact directly with customers and play an active role in service delivery.
  • 146. Process (7Ps): The internal workflow, structural steps, and automated systems involved in delivering a product or service efficiently to a customer.
  • 147. Physical Environment (7Ps): The tangible, visible marketing environment including layout, branding, and staff attire where service interaction takes place.
  • 148. Selling: A narrow, production-oriented approach viewing the customer as the last link, focusing purely on transferring title and possession for a price.
  • 149. Time Utility: Created primarily by warehousing functions, preserving goods safely during the lag between production and eventual consumption.
  • 150. Place Utility: Created primarily by transportation functions, physically moving goods to locations where they are demanded by consumers.
  • 151. Form Utility: Created during the production process by altering the shape or form of raw materials into a finished, consumable product.
  • 152. Market Planning: Creating a blueprint defining marketing objectives and actionable strategies required to achieve those desired market outcomes.
  • 153. Promotional Channels: A blend of tools like advertising, publicity, and personal selling used to communicate and induce customers to buy.
  • 154. Consumer Satisfaction: The ultimate goal of modern marketing, achieved when an offering completely meets or exceeds the customer's expectations.
  • 155. Information Technology in Marketing: The use of E-Commerce, websites, and digital apps defining the new 'Space Concept' of modern digital markets.
  • 156. Brand Equity: The overall value, trust, and strong reputation a business builds in the market through effective, continuous marketing campaigns.
  • 157. Target Market: The specific segment of potential consumers identified through market research at which an organization directs its marketing efforts.
  • 158. After-Sales Service: Assistance provided to a consumer post-purchase to resolve issues, which is critical for creating trust and brand loyalty.
  • 159. Unregulated Market: A free market characterized by the absence of government controls, operating purely on the natural forces of demand and supply.
  • 160. Tangible Product Features: Physical attributes of a product evaluated from a marketing perspective, such as color, shape, music system, and seats in a car.
  • 161. Intangible Product Features: Non-physical benefits attached to a product, such as the comfort, status, or servicing frequency provided to the buyer.
