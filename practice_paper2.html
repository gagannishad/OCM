Chapter 2: Functions of Management

Management is a set of principles relating to various functions such as planning, organizing, staffing, directing, coordinating, and controlling which are helpful in achieving organizational goals.[cite: 4] According to L.A. Allen, "Management is what a manager does."[cite: 4] Luther Gullick gave the keyword POSDCORB representing Planning, Organizing, Staffing, Directing, Co-ordination, Reporting, and Budgeting.[cite: 4]

1. Planning

Meaning & Definition: Planning is the basic function of management. Every other function is based on it. It involves deciding in advance what to do, how to do it, when to do it, and who is to do it.[cite: 4] It is an intellectual process of logical thinking.[cite: 4] Koontz and O'Donnell define it as bridging the gap from where we are to where we want to go.[cite: 4]

  • Helps to set clear objectives: Analyzes the present condition to identify ways to attain desired future positions.[cite: 4]
  • Provides path of action: Acts as a guide and provides clear direction for doing the right things at the right time.[cite: 4]
  • Improves performance: Enables employees to work according to timely guidelines, increasing profitability.[cite: 4]
  • Minimizes risk: Based on forecasting, it anticipates future changes and tackles them with preventive measures.[cite: 4]
  • Optimum utilization of resources: Ensures proper allocation of men, money, and material, avoiding wastage.[cite: 4]
  • Helps in decision making: Selects the best alternative by considering all positive and negative outcomes.[cite: 4]
  • Sets standards for controlling: Provides the benchmarks against which actual performance is compared.[cite: 4]
  • Facilitates coordination: Inter-relates all activities so departments work as per the overall plan.[cite: 4]
  • Facilitates other functions: Organizing and staffing cannot happen unless the plan is ready.[cite: 4]
  • Promotes innovative ideas: Decision-making promotes out-of-the-box thinking and creative strategies.[cite: 4]
2. Organizing

Meaning & Definition: The process of identifying and bringing together required resources (men, money, material, machine, method), grouping them, and arranging them properly to achieve objectives.[cite: 4] Theo Haimann defines it as defining and grouping activities and establishing authority relationships.[cite: 4]

  • Facilitates Administration and Operation: Grouping tasks aids administration while proper assignment aids operations.[cite: 4]
  • Brings Specialization: Dividing work into units brings specialization, increasing organizational efficiency.[cite: 4]
  • Defines Jobs Properly: Selects people based on qualifications, skills, and experience for specific jobs.[cite: 4]
  • Clarifies Authority and Responsibility: Delegates authority and clarifies powers to prevent misuse and increase productivity.[cite: 4]
  • Establishes Coordination: Creates clear relationships among positions ensuring mutual cooperation.[cite: 4]
  • Helps for Effective Administration: Determines positions, departments, and activities clearly.[cite: 4]
  • Helpful for Growth and Diversification: Smooth functioning and defined roles allow the business to scale.[cite: 4]
  • Creates Sense of Security: Clear duties and powers give employees mental satisfaction and professional stability.[cite: 4]
  • Scope for Innovation: Clear roles give managers space to develop talent and adopt new methods.[cite: 4]
  • Optimum Utilization of Resources: The right workforce utilizes physical and financial resources perfectly.[cite: 4]
3. Staffing

Meaning & Definition: The function of execution according to the organizational structure. It is the process of attracting, recruiting, selecting, placing, appraising, remunerating, developing, and retaining the best workforce.[cite: 4] The basic principle is "Right person at right job with right pay."[cite: 4]

  • Effective Managerial Function: It is the key to the effective performance of planning, organizing, directing, and controlling.[cite: 4]
  • Effective Utilization of Human Resources: Proper care at every stage prevents excessive work burdens.[cite: 4]
  • Builds Cordial Relationship: Healthy human relations lead to better communication and coordination.[cite: 4]
  • Human Resource Development: Trains and develops the workforce, treating skilled employees as an asset.[cite: 4]
  • Effective use of Technology: Trained employees can maximize the output from the latest machinery and methods.[cite: 4]
  • Improves Efficiency: Training programs directly enhance the quality and quantity of employee performance.[cite: 4]
  • Long Term Effect: A highly skilled workforce leads the organization towards long-term success.[cite: 4]
  • Essential Contribution: Selects candidates with future potential to meet upcoming business challenges.[cite: 4]
  • Provides Job Satisfaction: Fair remuneration and non-financial incentives boost morale.[cite: 4]
  • Maintains Harmony: Unbiased performance appraisals and merit-based promotions create a peaceful workplace.[cite: 4]
4. Directing

Meaning & Definition: The soul and 'life spark' of management. It is the continuous process of instructing, guiding, communicating, inspiring, motivating, and supervising employees.[cite: 4] It initiates action from the top level downwards.[cite: 4]

  • Initiates Action: Supports activating the plans by giving timely instructions to subordinates.[cite: 4]
  • Integrates Efforts: Communication integrates individual and departmental efforts into one organizational goal.[cite: 4]
  • Means of Motivation: Managers identify potential and offer incentives to encourage best performance.[cite: 4]
  • Provides Stability: Effective leadership and communication foster cooperation and commitment.[cite: 4]
  • Coping up with Changes: Helps employees understand and adapt to environmental and technological changes.[cite: 4]
  • Effective Utilization of Resources: Proper guidance avoids duplication of efforts and reduces costs.[cite: 4]
  • Creates Team Spirit: Focuses on group efforts and teamwork over isolated individual actions.[cite: 4]
  • Explores Capabilities: Identifies hidden talents and utilizes them through encouraging leadership.[cite: 4]
  • Increases Efficiency Level: Proper supervision techniques result in enhanced overall organizational efficiency.[cite: 4]
  • Co-operation: Created by the manager from top to bottom, necessary for smooth workflow.[cite: 4]
5. Co-ordinating

Meaning & Definition: The integration and synchronization of group efforts to provide unity of action.[cite: 4] It is a hidden force that binds all other functions. Henry Fayol defines it as harmonizing all activities to facilitate working and success.[cite: 4]

  • Encourages Team Spirit: Arranges work systematically to minimize conflicts and disputes.[cite: 4]
  • Gives Proper Direction: Interdependence of departments gives clear, unified direction to all employees.[cite: 4]
  • Facilitates Motivation: Coordination increases prosperity, which ensures job security and better pay, acting as motivation.[cite: 4]
  • Optimum Utilization of Resources: Systematic integration minimizes wastage and controls operating costs.[cite: 4]
  • Achieve Organizational Objectives: Reduces idle time and delays, ensuring goals are met on time.[cite: 4]
  • Improves Relation: Develops cordial relationships since every department (e.g., sales, production) depends on each other.[cite: 4]
  • Leads to Higher Efficiency: Achieves high returns in terms of productivity and profitability.[cite: 4]
  • Improves Goodwill: Lower costs and synchronized efforts build a strong corporate image in society.[cite: 4]
  • Unity of Direction: Harmonizes distinct departmental activities toward one common goal.[cite: 4]
  • Specialization: Combines the specialized knowledge of various experts into one harmonious whole.[cite: 4]
6. Controlling

Meaning & Definition: The indispensable end-function of management. It involves comparing actual performance with predetermined standards, measuring deviations, analyzing causes, and taking corrective measures.[cite: 4]

  • Fulfilling Goals: Measures performance at every stage and takes corrective actions to ensure goals are met.[cite: 4]
  • Efficient Utilization of Resources: Employees follow set standards, drastically reducing spoilage and material waste.[cite: 4]
  • Accuracy of Standards: Allows management to review and revise target standards based on changing circumstances.[cite: 4]
  • Motivates Employees: Advance knowledge of evaluation and corresponding rewards pushes employees to perform better.[cite: 4]
  • Ensures Order and Discipline: Continuous checking by superiors reduces unprofessional behavior.[cite: 4]
  • Facilitates Co-ordination: Clearly designed roles help find departmental deviations and apply targeted remedies.[cite: 4]
  • Psychological Pressure: Awareness of impending evaluation works as a powerful psychological motivator.[cite: 4]
  • Ensures Efficiency and Effectiveness: Makes managers responsible and achieves tight departmental coordination.[cite: 4]
  • Builds Corporate Image: Minimum deviation ensures quality, building immense goodwill and trust in the market.[cite: 4]
  • Acts as a Guide: The data and steps taken during control guide management while planning future cycles.[cite: 4]

Chapter 4: Business Services

Services are intangible activities, benefits, or satisfactions offered for sale. They are characterized by Intangibility (cannot be touched), Inseparability (production and consumption happen simultaneously), Inconsistency (heterogeneous quality), Perishability (cannot be stored as inventory), Non-transferability (ownership remains with the provider), and the need for Consumer Participation.[cite: 4]

1. Banking

A bank accepts deposits for the purpose of lending or investment. The RBI is the Central Bank of India. Commercial Banks perform primary functions (accepting Fixed, Recurring, Savings, and Current deposits; granting Loans, Cash Credit, Overdrafts, and discounting bills) and secondary functions (Agency functions like portfolio management and fund transfer; Utility functions like locker facilities and issuing drafts).[cite: 4] Other banks include Co-operative Banks, Development Banks (IFCI, SFC), Exchange Banks, Regional Rural Banks (RRBs), Savings Banks, Investment Banks, and Specialized Banks (EXIM, SIDBI, NABARD, Payment Banks).[cite: 4] E-banking includes ATMs, Credit/Debit cards, RTGS (real-time gross settlement, min Rs. 2 Lakhs), NEFT (deferred net basis), Net Banking, Mobile Banking, and IMPS.[cite: 4]

2. Insurance

A contract protecting against financial loss. The Principles of Insurance are: Utmost Good Faith (full disclosure), Insurable Interest (financial stake in the subject matter), Indemnity (restoring to exact prior financial position - not for life insurance), Subrogation (insurer claims ownership of salvage after payout), Contribution (multiple insurers share the loss proportionately), Mitigation of Loss (insured must try to save property), and Causa-Proxima (nearest direct cause determines liability).[cite: 4]

  • Life Insurance: Whole Life, Endowment, Term (lowest premium), Annuity, Money-back, Child, Retirement, ULIP.[cite: 4]
  • Marine Insurance: Voyage, Time, Mixed, Valued, Blanket, Port Risk, Composite, Single Vessel, Fleet, Block policy.[cite: 4]
  • Fire Insurance: Valued, Average (proportionate payout if under-insured), Specific, Floating (fluctuating locations), Excess, Reinstatement (physical replacement), Comprehensive, Consequential Loss, Sprinkler Leakage.[cite: 4]
3. Transport & Warehousing

Transport Modes: Road (door-to-door, cheap for short distances, limited capacity), Rail (dependable, huge capacity for long distance, lacks door-to-door), Air (fastest, expensive, vital for emergencies), Water (most economical for bulky international goods, slow), Monorail/Metro, Ropeways, Pipeline.[cite: 4]

Warehousing Functions: Storage, Price Stabilization, Risk bearing, Financing, Grading/Packing, Transportation, creating Time and Place Utility, Processing. Types: Private, Public, Bonded (stores imported goods until duty is paid), Duty Paid (goods stored after duty is paid), Government (CWC, FCI), Co-operative, Cold Storage (for perishables).[cite: 4]

4. Communication

The art of exchanging ideas. Postal Services: Mail (Inland letter, Envelope, Parcel, Book-Post), Specialized (Business Post, Logistics Post, Bill Mail Service), Remittance (eMO, iMO, International Transfer), Retail (Retail Post, e-Post). Modern Means: Courier Service, Internet, Email.[cite: 4]

Chapter 8: Marketing

Marketing is the process of identifying and fulfilling the needs of individuals through exchange.[cite: 4] The word market derives from the Latin 'mercatus' (to trade).[cite: 4]

1. Concepts & Types of Market

Concepts: Place (physical location), Commodity (focus on buying/selling goods), Exchange (voluntary exchange with trust), Area (price-fixing in a region), Demand/Customer (aggregate potential demand), Space/Digital (e-commerce).[cite: 4]

Types: By Area (Local, National, International); By Time (Very Short, Short, Long); By Volume (Wholesale, Retail); By Importance (Primary, Secondary, Terminal); By Nature (Commodity, Capital); By Regulation (Regulated, Unregulated); By Competition (Perfect, Imperfect - Monopoly [one seller], Duopoly [two sellers], Oligopoly [few sellers], Monopsony [one buyer]).[cite: 4]

2. Importance of Marketing
  • To Society: Increases standard of living, provides immense employment, decreases distribution costs, builds consumer awareness, increases national income, and manages consumer expectations.[cite: 4]
  • To the Firm: Increases awareness, drives sales and profits, creates long-term trust, forms a basis for complex decision making, acts as a source of new ideas (R&D), and helps effectively tackle competition.[cite: 4]
  • To Consumers: Promotes product awareness, forces firms to provide quality products, provides vast variety, helps in product selection, guarantees consumer satisfaction, and ensures a regular supply of goods.[cite: 4]
3. Functions of Marketing & The Marketing Mix

Functions: Marketing Research, Buying & Assembling, Market Planning, Product Development, Standardisation & Grading, Packaging & Labelling, Branding (Trademarks), Customer Support Service, Pricing, Promotional Channels, Distribution, Transportation, Warehousing.[cite: 4]

Marketing Mix (7Ps): Introduced by E. Jerome McCarthy as 4Ps, extended by Booms & Bitner to 7Ps for services.[cite: 4] Product (goods/services offered), Price (amount customer pays), Place (distribution channels and accessibility), Promotion (communication, advertising). For services: People (staff delivering service), Process (workflow and steps taken), Physical Environment (tangible layout and decor).[cite: 4]

PART B: Quick Chapter Summaries

Chapter 2 Summary: Management is a continuous cycle starting with Planning (deciding the future path), followed by Organizing (structuring resources), Staffing (hiring and training human capital), Directing (leading and motivating staff into action), Coordinating (harmonizing all departmental efforts), and ending with Controlling (evaluating actual performance against standards and applying corrective remedies).[cite: 4]

Chapter 4 Summary: Business services are essential, intangible aids to trade. Banking manages financial liquidity, loans, and modern e-transfers (RTGS, NEFT). Insurance acts as a risk hedge based on core principles (Indemnity, Utmost Good Faith, Subrogation) across Life, Fire, and Marine policies. Transport overcomes distance barriers through various modes. Warehousing overcomes time barriers by safely storing goods (Public, Private, Bonded). Communication links buyers and sellers via Postal and modern digital networks.[cite: 4]

Chapter 8 Summary: Marketing is a customer-oriented philosophy centered on consumer satisfaction, vastly differing from the narrow scope of Selling. Markets can be categorized by place, time, competition (Monopoly, Oligopoly), and volume. Marketing functions cover everything from initial Market Research to final Warehousing and After-sales support. The strategic blend of Product, Price, Place, Promotion, People, Process, and Physical Environment forms the critical Marketing Mix necessary for business survival.[cite: 4]

PART C: Practice Mock Paper 2

Time: 2 Hours Organisation of Commerce & Management Total Marks: 50
Q.1 (A) State whether the following statements are True or False: [05 Marks]
1. Controlling is the absolute first function of the management process. Answer: False. Planning is the first function; controlling is the last.
2. Payment banks are strictly prohibited from issuing loans and credit cards. Answer: True.
3. The Principle of Indemnity is fully applicable to Life Insurance contracts. Answer: False. It is not applicable to life insurance because a human life's monetary value cannot be measured.
4. Selling views the customer as the absolute primary purpose of the business. Answer: False. Marketing views the customer as the primary purpose; Selling views them as the last link.
5. Capital markets are designed exclusively for borrowing and lending long-term capital. Answer: True.
Q.1 (B) Find the odd one out: [05 Marks]
1. Planning, Organizing, Staffing, Manufacturing. Answer: Manufacturing
2. ATM, RTGS, Telegram, NEFT. Answer: Telegram
3. Endownment Policy, Whole Life Policy, Fire Policy, Money-back Policy. Answer: Fire Policy
4. Monopoly, Duopoly, Monopsony, Local Market. Answer: Local Market
5. Product, Price, People, Promotion. Answer: People (It belongs to the extended 3Ps of Service Marketing)
Q.2 Explain the following terms/concepts (Any 5 out of 6): [10 Marks]
  • 1. Staffing: The managerial function responsible for recruiting, selecting, training, compensating, and retaining the right workforce.
  • 2. Co-ordinating: The integration and synchronization of departmental efforts to ensure unity of action towards common goals.
  • 3. Bonded Warehouse: Government-licensed storage facilities used to hold imported goods securely until custom duties are cleared by the importer.
  • 4. RTGS: Real Time Gross Settlement; an electronic banking facility for transferring funds instantaneously on a gross basis (minimum limit Rs. 2 Lakhs).
  • 5. Marketing Mix: The strategic blend of 7 controllable variables (Product, Price, Place, Promotion, People, Process, Physical Environment) used by a firm to influence a target market.
  • 6. Monopsony: A unique imperfect market structure characterized by the existence of a single powerful buyer interacting with multiple sellers.
Q.3 Study the following case/situation and express your opinion (Any 2 out of 3): [06 Marks]
Case 1:

Mr. Sharma's factory was insured for Rs. 50 Lakhs. A devastating fire broke out, causing a total loss of Rs. 20 Lakhs. Mr. Sharma claimed Rs. 50 Lakhs from the insurance company because that was his policy limit.

i. Can Mr. Sharma successfully claim Rs. 50 Lakhs?
Answer: No, he cannot claim Rs. 50 Lakhs.

ii. Which principle of insurance restricts his claim?
Answer: The Principle of Indemnity.

iii. How much compensation is he actually entitled to receive?
Answer: He is entitled to receive only the actual loss suffered, which is Rs. 20 Lakhs.

Case 2:

A prominent tech firm notices its employees are highly demotivated. The manager decides to conduct one-on-one sessions, offers transparent guidance, and promises performance-linked financial bonuses.

i. Which management function is the manager executing?
Answer: The manager is executing the Directing function.

ii. What specific elements of this function are being utilized?
Answer: Leadership, Communication, and Motivation.

iii. Why is this function critical at this stage?
Answer: Because it initiates action, boosts morale, and puts pre-planned strategies into motion.

Case 3:

Farmer Kishan separates his harvested mangoes by size and quality. He packs the premium ones in attractive wooden boxes and puts a sticker reading 'Kishan Premium' on them before distributing them nationwide.

i. What is the process of separating mangoes by quality called?
Answer: Grading.

ii. Putting the mangoes in attractive wooden boxes relates to which function?
Answer: Packaging.

iii. Naming the product 'Kishan Premium' is an example of what?
Answer: Branding.

Q.4 Distinguish between (Any 4 out of 5): [16 Marks]
  • 1. Planning and Controlling: Planning looks ahead to set goals and standards; Controlling looks backward to measure performance against those standards and correct deviations.
  • 2. Commercial Bank and Central Bank: Central Bank (RBI) regulates the economy, issues currency, and does not deal with the public. Commercial Banks accept public deposits and grant business loans.
  • 3. Life Insurance and Fire Insurance: Life insurance deals with human life (indemnity principle does not apply, long-term). Fire insurance covers property damage (indemnity principle strictly applies, short-term).
  • 4. Marketing and Selling: Marketing is a broad, customer-oriented approach aiming for consumer satisfaction. Selling is a narrow, production-oriented approach aiming strictly at transferring ownership for cash.
  • 5. Current Account and Fixed Deposit: Current account is a demand deposit with no transaction limits, used by businesses. Fixed Deposit locks a specific sum for a set period, offering a high interest rate.
Q.5 Answer the following in detail (Any 1 out of 2): [08 Marks]
1. What is Warehousing? Explain any six functions of Warehouses.
Meaning: Warehousing refers to the establishment and activities connected with the safe storing and preserving of goods from production till consumption, creating time utility.
Functions:
1. Storage: Safely accumulates surplus stock.
2. Price Stabilization: Prevents drastic price fluctuations by balancing supply and demand.
3. Risk Bearing: Protects goods from fire, theft, and deterioration.
4. Financing: Allows business owners to raise bank loans by pledging warehouse receipts.
5. Grading & Packing: Provides facilities to sort, process, and package goods into convenient sizes.
6. Time & Place Utility: Bridges the gap between production and consumption seasons, delivering goods to required locations.
2. Define Marketing. Explain the importance of Marketing to the Firm and to the Consumers.
Definition: According to Philip Kotler, "Marketing is a societal process by which individuals and groups obtain what they need and want through creating, offering and freely exchanging products..."
Importance to the Firm:
1. Increases product awareness and builds brand trust.
2. Generates higher sales volumes, leading to increased profitability.
3. Provides vital market data serving as a basis for strategic decision-making.
4. Acts as a source of new ideas for continuous R&D and product development.
Importance to Consumers:
1. Promotes awareness of new product features and utility.
2. Forces market competition, resulting in high-quality products and lower prices.
3. Provides a wide variety of choices, aiding in better product selection.
4. Focuses ultimately on maximizing consumer satisfaction.
