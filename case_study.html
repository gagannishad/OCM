PART A: Textbook Exercises

Chapter 2: Functions of Management

  • Case 1: Mr. Ram's Emerging Enterprise
    Mr. Ram, an emerging entrepreneur, has designed a structure for his business organization by taking into consideration the required resources such as land, money, machinery, workforce etc., for his new business. He appointed Mr. Shyam as a manager. Mr. Ram has assigned responsibilities such as recruitment, selection, training and development, and determining the remuneration of employees to Mr. Shyam. Mr. Ram also appointed Mr. Shubham to supervise the work done by the employees according to the standards given to them. Mr. Shubham also suggests remedies to the employees wherever necessary.
    i. Find out the management function performed by Mr. Ram. Answer: Organizing. ii. Find out the management function performed by Mr. Shyam. Answer: Staffing. iii. Find out the management function performed by Mr. Shubham. Answer: Controlling.
  • Case 2: XYZ Company
    In XYZ Company, Mr. Lele gives instructions to the employees working under him, provides guidance, and motivates them for their best performance. On the other hand, Mr. Sayyad takes effort to harmonize the work done by the employees of different departments while achieving the organizational goal. Mr. Desai is looking after the arrangement of required resources for the business organization.
    i. Mention the name of the employee engaged in the Organization function. Answer: Mr. Desai. ii. Mention the name of the employee engaged in the Direction function. Answer: Mr. Lele. iii. Mention the name of the employee engaged in the Coordination function. Answer: Mr. Sayyad.

Chapter 4: Business Services

  • Case 3: Ms. Harshali's Business Account
    Ms. Harshali started a new business two years ago. Her customers are located in different parts of the country and hence they directly deposit bill amounts into her business account. At the same time, she uses this account to make various payments.
    i. Identify the type of account maintained by Ms. Harshali. Answer: Current Account. ii. Suggest any one modern way of money transfer to Ms. Harshali. Answer: RTGS / NEFT / IMPS. iii. What kind of facility does she get on her bank account? Answer: Overdraft Facility.
  • Case 4: Mr. Jagan's Policy Decision
    Mr. Jagan is a salaried person. He wants to take a policy for his two children which assures them protection as well as completes their financial needs once they become majors by age.
    i. Suggest a policy which can satisfy the requirements of his children. Answer: Child Insurance Policy. ii. Who are the beneficiaries of the policy? Answer: The two children. iii. In the above case, which principle of insurance is involved? Answer: Principle of Insurable Interest.
  • Case 5: Mr. Sharan's Manufacturing Units
    Mr. Sharan is a successful manufacturer having production units at various locations. He has a large stock of raw materials and finished goods and is worried about safeguarding goods from unwanted financial loss. He also requires transferring raw materials and finished goods from one unit to another but lacks facility. He also requires funds for expansion.
    i. Name the service which will help him safeguard goods from any damage. Answer: Insurance and Warehousing. ii. Which service will help him remove the difficulty of place? Answer: Transport Service. iii. From which service sector will he get financial support? Answer: Banking Service.
  • Case 6: Mr. Amit's Factories
    Mr. Amit is a businessman having factories in Pune and Nashik. He lives in Pune with his wife and 2 daughters aged 5 and 8 years.
    i. Can Mr. Amit take a life insurance policy for his wife and 2 children? Answer: Yes, because he has an insurable interest in their lives. ii. Can Mr. Amit take a marine insurance policy for his factories? Answer: No, marine insurance covers loss or damage to vessels or cargo during sea transit, not stationary factories. iii. Which type of insurance should Mr. Amit take to protect his factories from fire? Answer: Fire Insurance (Comprehensive or Specific Policy).

Chapter 8: Marketing

  • Case 7: Purchasing Locations
    Mr. X purchases goods from the nearest shop. Mr. Y purchases mobiles from Tokyo. Mr. Z of Nandurbar purchases electronic goods from Delhi.
    i. From which type of market does Mr. X purchase? Answer: Local Market / Retail Market. ii. Name the type of market from where Mr. Y deals. Answer: International Market. iii. State the type of market from where Mr. Z purchases goods. Answer: National Market.
  • Case 8: Mr. X's Financial Deals
    Mr. X deals in the import and export business, so he needs different foreign currencies. For the expansion of his business, he borrows money from a bank. He also invests his funds in equity shares.
    i. Name the market from where Mr. X borrows money. Answer: Capital Market (for long term) or Money Market. ii. Name the market where Mr. X invests his funds. Answer: Stock Market / Secondary Capital Market. iii. Which type of currency is required for the international market? Answer: Foreign Exchange / Forex.

PART B: Extensive Custom Scenarios

Chapter 2: Functions of Management

  • Case 9: The Disorganized Startup
    Rohan started a tech company. He hired 20 people but nobody knows who reports to whom. The marketing team is doing sales, and the sales team is sitting idle. Rohan finally decides to group activities logically, create departments, and assign specific authority to department heads.
    i. Which management function was missing initially? Answer: Organizing. ii. What benefit will Rohan get by grouping activities? Answer: It brings specialization and avoids duplication of work. iii. What does assigning authority to department heads achieve? Answer: It clarifies authority and responsibility.
  • Case 10: The Visionary Leader
    Sunita is the CEO of a retail chain. Before the financial year starts, she sits with her board to decide the sales targets for the next year, maps out the steps required to achieve them, and anticipates future risks in the retail sector to prepare backup strategies.
    i. Identify the management function Sunita is performing. Answer: Planning. ii. Why is anticipating future risks important here? Answer: It minimizes the risk of uncertain future events. iii. What is the fundamental nature of this function? Answer: It is an intellectual process of logical thinking and decision making.
  • Case 11: Bridging the Gap
    In a car manufacturing plant, the target was to produce 1,000 cars a month. At the end of the month, only 850 cars were produced. Manager Karan investigates the issue, finds that machine breakdowns caused the delay, and immediately orders new maintenance schedules to ensure the target is met next month.
    i. Which management function is Karan executing? Answer: Controlling. ii. What does the difference of 150 cars represent? Answer: Deviation from the standard performance. iii. What is the final step Karan took in this process? Answer: Taking corrective action.
  • Case 12: Harmonizing the Teams
    The Production manager is producing 500 units a day, but the Sales manager can only sell 300 units a day, leading to massive unsold stock. The General Manager steps in and aligns the production schedule strictly with the sales forecasts to ensure smooth flow without wastage.
    i. Which management function is the General Manager performing? Answer: Co-ordinating. ii. What was the outcome of the lack of this function initially? Answer: Wastage of resources and overproduction. iii. How does this function help the organization? Answer: It integrates efforts and brings unity of direction.
  • Case 13: Inspiring the Workforce
    A team of software developers is feeling demotivated due to long hours. The project lead, Sameer, conducts a meeting, actively listens to their issues, provides clear guidance on the coding structure, and promises performance bonuses for finishing the project on time.
    i. Which management function is Sameer actively performing? Answer: Directing. ii. Which specific elements of this function are used here? Answer: Leadership, Communication, and Motivation. iii. Why is this function called the 'life spark' of an enterprise? Answer: Because it initiates action and puts plans into motion.
  • Case 14: HR Crisis
    A hospital is facing a severe shortage of nurses. The HR head, Dr. Priya, immediately releases an advertisement, interviews candidates, selects the best ones, and arranges a one-week orientation program to train them on hospital protocols.
    i. Identify the management function Dr. Priya is handling. Answer: Staffing. ii. What is the basic principle she must follow? Answer: Right person at the right job with the right pay. iii. What is the purpose of the orientation program? Answer: Training and human resource development.

Chapter 4: Business Services

  • Case 15: The Unforeseen Fire
    Mr. Gupta insured his warehouse valued at Rs. 10 Lakhs for only Rs. 5 Lakhs. A fire breaks out and destroys goods worth Rs. 4 Lakhs. The insurance company pays him only Rs. 2 Lakhs. Mr. Gupta is confused as to why the full loss of 4 Lakhs wasn't covered.
    i. Which fire insurance policy clause applied here? Answer: Average Policy / Average Clause. ii. Why did the insurance company pay only Rs. 2 Lakhs? Answer: Because the property was under-insured by 50%, so only 50% of the loss is compensated. iii. Which overarching principle prevents him from making a profit out of a loss? Answer: Principle of Indemnity.
  • Case 16: The Rapid Funds Need
    ABC Traders urgently needs to send Rs. 5 Lakhs to a supplier in another state within the next hour to release a critical shipment. Normal cheque clearance takes two days. They approach their bank for the fastest electronic method.
    i. Which modern banking facility should ABC Traders use? Answer: RTGS (Real Time Gross Settlement). ii. Why is this facility appropriate here? Answer: It settles transactions in real-time without a waiting period. iii. What is the minimum limit for this specific transaction method? Answer: Rs. Two Lakhs.
  • Case 17: Import Dilemma
    Mr. David imports luxury cars into India. A shipment arrives at the Mumbai port, but he is currently short on funds to pay the heavy custom duties immediately. He needs a secure place to store the cars until he can arrange the funds.
    i. Where should Mr. David store his imported cars? Answer: Bonded Warehouse. ii. Who licenses and controls these specific warehouses? Answer: The Government and Custom Authorities. iii. When can Mr. David withdraw his cars from this warehouse? Answer: Only after paying the required custom duties.
  • Case 18: Multiple Insurers
    Karan takes a fire insurance policy for his factory worth Rs. 50 Lakhs from Company A for 30 Lakhs, and from Company B for 20 Lakhs. A fire causes a loss of Rs. 10 Lakhs. Karan claims Rs. 10 Lakhs from Company A and Rs. 10 Lakhs from Company B, expecting a total of 20 Lakhs.
    i. Will Karan receive Rs. 20 Lakhs in total? Answer: No. ii. Which principle of insurance prevents this? Answer: Principle of Contribution. iii. How much is he actually entitled to receive in total? Answer: He can only receive the actual loss amount, i.e., Rs. 10 Lakhs total, contributed proportionately by A and B.
  • Case 19: The Perishable Export
    FarmFresh India needs to export highly perishable exotic fruits to London within 24 hours to ensure they don't spoil. They are evaluating the best mode of transport and the best storage facility before the flight.
    i. Which mode of transport is mandatory for this requirement? Answer: Air Transport. ii. What type of warehouse should they use before loading? Answer: Cold Storage Warehouse. iii. What is the major disadvantage of this mode of transport? Answer: It is a relatively very expensive mode of transport.
  • Case 20: The Corporate Mailer
    A telecom company needs to send printed monthly bills to 50,000 customers across the state. They want a cost-effective postal solution specially designed for corporate bulk mailings that are purely financial statements.
    i. Which specialized postal service should the telecom company use? Answer: Bill Mail Service. ii. What is the minimum quantity of articles required to use this service? Answer: 5000 articles. iii. Can they get a credit facility for this service? Answer: No, there is no credit facility for Bill Mail Service.

Chapter 8: Marketing

  • Case 21: The Unbranded Grain
    Farmer Kishan produces high-quality wheat. He currently sells it loose in the local market. A consultant advises him to separate the wheat by size and quality, pack it in strong attractive 5kg bags, and give it a unique name like 'Kishan Gold' before selling.
    i. What is the process of separating the wheat by quality called? Answer: Grading. ii. Putting the wheat in attractive 5kg bags is an example of which function? Answer: Packaging. iii. Giving it the name 'Kishan Gold' represents which marketing function? Answer: Branding.
  • Case 22: The Airline Strategy
    SkyHigh Airlines is launching a new route. They ensure their airplanes are spotless, their crew is highly trained to be polite and efficient, and their check-in workflow is entirely automated via an app to save time for passengers.
    i. Training the crew to be polite relates to which 'P' of the service marketing mix? Answer: People. ii. The automated check-in workflow relates to which 'P'? Answer: Process. iii. The spotless airplane interior relates to which 'P'? Answer: Physical Environment (Physical Evidence).
  • Case 23: Setting the Right Cost
    A company launched a highly innovative smartphone. However, they priced it so high that nobody bought it, and it failed. They re-launched it after doing extensive research on what customers are willing to pay and adjusting the cost slightly above the competitor's rate.
    i. Which element of the marketing mix did the company fail to execute properly initially? Answer: Price. ii. What function did they perform to find out customer willingness? Answer: Marketing Research. iii. What is the risk of pricing a product too low instead? Answer: It might severely affect the profitability of the business.
  • Case 24: Market Structures
    In City A, there is only one electricity provider, and citizens have no other choice. In City B, there are exactly two major internet providers who dominate the entire market. In City C, hundreds of farmers are selling identical tomatoes at the exact same price.
    i. City A represents which type of market competition? Answer: Monopoly. ii. City B represents which type of market competition? Answer: Duopoly. iii. City C represents which type of market competition? Answer: Perfect Market.
  • Case 25: The Ultimate Goal
    Company Alpha believes that just producing high-quality shoes efficiently will make them successful. Company Beta believes in identifying what style of shoes teenagers want, offering fair prices, honest advertising, and a strong return policy.
    i. Company Alpha is following which type of approach? Answer: Production-oriented (Selling approach). ii. Company Beta is following which type of approach? Answer: Customer-oriented (Marketing approach). iii. What is the ultimate outcome Company Beta is aiming for through its policies? Answer: Consumer Satisfaction.
